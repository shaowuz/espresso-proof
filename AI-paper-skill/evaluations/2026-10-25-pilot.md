# Three-excerpt behavioral pilot — 2026-10-25

This is a small, purposive AI-reviewed pilot of the updated instruction set.
It is not a human readability study or a mathematical correctness benchmark.
The machine-readable [record](2026-10-25-pilot.json) contains source URLs, hashes,
masked assignments, instruction hashes, review provenance, and limitations.

## Method

Two fresh writer contexts received the same task and source material. One used
an ordinary readability-rewrite prompt; the other additionally read the frozen
updated skill and its relevant references. Both were restricted to the supplied
excerpts, with no external source checking. The configured model was inherited;
the exact provider model ID and sampling settings were not exposed. Each condition
ran once on each of three passages.

The originals were taken from the public OpenAI mathematics collection at commit
1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a. The primary agent checked PDF extraction
against rendered pages and normalized mathematical notation into LaTeX. This
checked transcription, not the truth of the source theorems.

Manuscript outputs were separated from writer notes and relabeled A/B with the
assignment varied across cases before generation. A fresh reviewer saw only the
original passages and masked manuscripts. The condition key, writer notes, and
skill instructions were withheld. The reviewer assessed preservation first, then
readability, and recorded its judgments before the key was revealed.

## Observed results

| Case | Material | Masked decision | Decoded outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E01 | Milne rationality paper, page 1: two introductory paragraphs | Tie | No material preference |
| E02 | Low-Selmer-corank BSD paper, page 3: conventions and Theorem 1.1 | Tie | No material preference |
| E03 | Catalan paper, page 3: normalized bounds and the common sequence | A | Modest preference for the skill-assisted rewrite |

In E03, the skill-assisted rewrite made the unbounded sequence already stated in
the supplied context explicit, then connected both asymptotic estimates to it.
The reviewer described this as a small editorial improvement, not a mathematical
defect in the baseline. It reported no introduced mathematical defect in the six
rewrites. That finding is limited to what this AI reviewer detected.

Do not turn these observations into an estimated general win rate or claim that
the skill has been shown to improve full papers. The two ties are part of the
result. Seven-metric percentages were not fabricated from global preferences.

## Limits and evidence retained

- Three selected excerpts, one generation per condition, and one AI reviewer.
- Same configured model family; correlated errors and stylistic preferences remain.
- Masked condition labels do not prevent a reviewer inferring a condition from style.
- No human review, inter-rater reliability measurement, formal verification, or
  certification of the advanced source claims and references.
- The sources include already polished exposition; this is not a representative
  sample of all AI-generated mathematical writing or of difficult proof repairs.
- Prompts, PDFs, transcriptions, source/skill/output hashes, writer notes, masked
  review, and the assignment key are retained in a local ignored evaluation archive.
  This public summary does not redistribute the source excerpts or raw rewrites.
- The evaluated instruction snapshot was frozen before generation. After review,
  a pilot-report cross-link and scoring-helper type-validation fix were added;
  the manuscript-rewriting instructions were not edited.

The next stronger evaluation would use additional held-out papers, repeated runs,
and independent human readers, while separately tracking preservation and newly
introduced errors. The present pilot supplies bounded behavioral evidence only.
